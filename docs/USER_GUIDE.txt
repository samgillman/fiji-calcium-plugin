Ca²⁺ IMAGING (WANG LAB)
A plugin for Fiji / ImageJ: calcium imaging, cells step by step.  Version 2026.11.23.1.

STEP 4 — LOCAL SIMPLECA ANALYSIS
After Measure and save, press Open analysis page. Step 4 opens as a page in your browser, served
by Fiji on your own computer. No recording data is uploaded. Keep Fiji open while using the page.
Use a Fiji window instead opens the native fallback. Both views include linked recording playback
and use the same R engine and saved analysis results. The browser adds interactive trace hover,
table sorting and an interactive heatmap.

The browser starts in Recording view. Play, the frame slider, previous/next frame and Speed are
directly under the picture and work before analysis or R setup. The page identifies this as a preview;
response-percentage tiles and result tables stay hidden until an analysis has completed.
Playback follows the available frame times. Unconfirmed seconds are labeled provisional. With
no valid time base, the page labels a preview fallback of 20 frames per second at 1x; this is a
display rate, not an acquisition-rate estimate. Static colour overview is explicitly a still image:
Play is disabled there, and frame controls are disabled before results exist. After analysis, the
frame slider can inspect the trace cursor without animating that overview. Choose Recording to
play or scrub movie frames.

On first use, click Set up analysis engine (internet required). This installs private R and
jsonlite for 64-bit Windows, Intel/Apple Silicon Mac or x64 Linux. Later analysis can run offline.
Confirm the frame rate or load exact per-frame timestamps. Choose the normalization baseline
frames, then review each protocol row's local baseline, stimulus onset and response window.
The primary stimulus and optional named control can have different thresholds and timing.
Detailed response rules, protocol files and experiment/diagnostic settings expand when needed;
the current timing and rule summary remain visible. Browser percentage fields use 10 for 10%,
which is stored as the fraction 0.10 in the shared protocol.
The starting rule is a rise of at least 10 % and at least 3 times the cell's noise, held for
2 samples in a row. Validate it on your controls. Noise is the larger of two estimates: the SD of
the baseline frames, and the whole-trace noise (1.4826 x the median absolute deviation of the
differences between consecutive frames, divided by the square root of 2). A baseline of a few frames
can come out quieter than the cell is; the second estimate keeps such a cell from being called on
two chance samples. A recording opened again has its last timing and thresholds filled in and takes
noise the same new way; the status line then says "Noise is now the larger of baseline SD and
whole-trace noise." Imported protocols and saved analyses keep the noise they were saved with (the
baseline SD alone). The Fiji window shows the noise in use under the protocol name. The page does
not show it yet; when remembered settings on the baseline SD are filled in, its status line ends
"Noise: baseline SD, as saved." Each analysis records which noise was used: sd_estimator in
settings.tsv and analysis_manifest.csv, noise_rule in the manifest and, with the whole-trace
estimate, Noise_source for every scored cell in responder_calls.csv.
A length given in seconds is the last qualifying timestamp minus the first: at 2 Hz, one second
needs three samples. The actual historical consecutive-sample rule remains available as a legacy
protocol; it retains its original endpoint handling.

Step 4 uses full-precision UNLIFTED background-corrected fluorescence. Invalid normalization,
missing data and incomplete stimulus coverage remain visible as exclusions or unscorable calls.
Percent responding is 100 * Responded / Evaluable; exclusions do not become nonresponders.
Draft edits and changed timestamp/diagnostic files are marked as pending. They do not relabel an
older completed result, its plots or its denominator, or change its movie timing and exports.
Apply changes & reanalyze runs the new settings; the last completed result remains until success.

In the browser, select a cell by trace, table, heatmap or picture. The time slider, Play button and
plot cursor move together in Recording view. Change from baseline subtracts the normalization
baseline image and lightly smooths the view. Static colour overview stays still. These are display
operations only. Filters, palettes and 99-percent display ranges do not change measurements.
The Step 3 intensity-lifted "every cell kept" table below remains a legacy diagnostic export;
Step 4 never uses it for fractional response percentages.

Protocol JSON, exact inputs, timestamps, ROI identities, exclusions, quality flags, window metrics,
biological-unit metadata and the applied settings are saved with each analysis. Optional sensitivity
and negative-control diagnostics stay separate from the applied result. See docs/SIMPLECA.md for
setup, formulas, full output definitions and reproducibility details.

CHECKS ON THE CALLS
After every analysis each scored cell is checked, and the result is saved beside the percent table
as ..._call_checks_<analysis>.csv, one row per cell. A check is a flag for you to look at in the
movie. It never changes a call, a count or a percentage.

  possible passing object, frames 201 to 210
        A bright object was outside the outline on one side just before the cell rose and outside
        on another side just after, and was gone in between. The frames are those of the rise.
  rise on the lower right edge, not in the cell, frames 92 to 149
        The rise is in the ring just outside one edge and not in the deeper half of the outline.
        It can be an object resting on the edge, or a neighbour without an outline.
  field moved, frames 28 to 35
        One of the two flags above would have been written, but from 10 seconds before the rise to
        10 seconds after it the field was 8 pixels or more from where it was over the F0 frames,
        or moved 8 pixels or more between two of those frames. A field that moves gives the same
        picture as an object, so the row names the movement and the frames it was seen in.
  short separate crossings
        A responder whose rise reached the rise it needs 3 or more separate times in the response
        window, and never for more than 2 samples in a row.
A stretch of one frame reads "frame 28".

The first three use what Measure and save measures around every cell. How, is under Around each
cell in HOW THE PICTURES AND NUMBERS ARE MADE. If those files could not be saved at Measure, or were
taken away since, they are measured when the analysis runs, with the same code, while the
recording is open in Fiji and gives the saved gray values. Otherwise the passing-object check is
not made, and every row says "not checked" and why. A recording measured with an earlier version
has no such files: measure it again to have it checked. Without the field movement file the table
is made, and the message says that a moving field is not told from an object.

A cell that is not checked says why in the column Not_checked_because, for example "no peak time"
or "no ring around the cell". Once the engine has finished, the analysis can no longer be
cancelled: the checks run to the end.

What the check cannot see:
  - It looks only at the largest rise of each cell. An object that makes the call while the
    largest rise is elsewhere is not seen.
  - On the nine recordings it was set on, it flagged 9 of the 14 responders that were objects by
    eye: 8 of 9 in outlines of 900 pixels or more, 1 of 5 in outlines under 700 pixels.
  - It misses most objects that cross an outline in 2 frames, and objects that come to rest inside
    the outline and stay.
  - A zone with fewer than 12 pixels is not used, for example a sector mostly covered by other
    outlines or cut by the picture's edge. The table gives the number of usable near-ring sectors
    of 8 and the outline's area for every cell.
  - A flag is not proof. Of 296 ATP responders 2 were flagged, and neither was an object.
A cell without a flag is not shown to be free of objects. The page does not show these flags yet;
they are in the table.

REVIEWING THE CALLS
A cell the engine scored can be marked the other way after you have looked at it: a responder as
not a responder, a non-responder as a responder. Each mark needs a reason: Noise, Movement, Passing
object, Bad outline or Other. Other needs a short note. A note is one line of plain text, up to 120
characters, and cannot start with =, +, - or @, which a spreadsheet would read as a formula. A mark
can be removed. Excluded and unscorable cells have no call and cannot be marked. The page does not
have the control yet; the marks are kept and counted by the plugin.

  Where       ..._reviewed_calls_<analysis>.csv beside the percent table, one row per mark: the
              cell, the automatic call, the reviewed call, the reason, the note, the time to the
              second and, for a mark carried over, the analysis it was made in. The file is there
              only while there is at least one mark. The engine's responder_calls.csv and the
              analysis folder are never changed.
  Counted     % responding is given as scored and after review, for all cells, the cells chosen
              blind, those added later, those carried over and the quiet cells, with and without
              the cells below zero.
              ..._percent_responding_<analysis>.csv keeps its rows and columns and gains five on
              the right while there are marks: responded_after_review,
              percent_responding_after_review, responded_without_them_after_review,
              percent_without_them_after_review and cells_reclassified. With no marks it is as
              before. The finish message gives both figures, and follows every mark; so does the
              Fiji window, which reads the marks when it loads results and then names its groups
              "Responded after review" and "Did not respond after review".
  Saved       A mark is saved together with the percent table, or not at all. If the percent table
              is open in another program, close it and set the mark again.
  Methods     A sentence from the marks, for example "3 of 143 cells were reclassified after
              manual review: 2 from responder to non-responder (1 noise, 1 passing object) and 1
              from non-responder to responder (other)."
  Re-run      When a new analysis of the same recording finishes, the marks of the most recent
              earlier analysis are carried over for every cell whose automatic call and outline
              are unchanged. The others are dropped, and the finish message lists them, for
              example "Cell 69: now scored as did not respond", "Cell 12 of the earlier analysis:
              no longer measured", "Cell 4: outline changed". The earlier analysis keeps its own
              review file. If you removed the marks of the most recent analysis, none are carried,
              and older marks do not come back.
  Checked     Every row names its analysis and the SHA-256 of the responder_calls.csv it was made
              on. A review file that does not match its analysis is not used, and the plugin says
              so; move it out of the folder to review that analysis again.

WHAT IT DOES
One menu command takes a calcium-imaging recording (a time-lapse movie, for example .zvi, .czi or .tif)
from the raw file to a table of gray values per cell:

  1. You choose the recording and a folder for the results.
  2. It makes the cells visible: cells are green and very bright objects (precipitate, debris)
     are yellow. Step 1 asks how cells are chosen:
       - Before the response only (blind), the default and the choice for % responding. The plugin
         offers the frames and you confirm them: they end 25 frames before the rise it finds, or at
         frame 60 when it finds no response, and they leave out frames at the start in which the
         field moved. They need not end where the stimulus was added (a drug can reach the chamber
         long before most cells respond); Step 4 asks for that separately. The picture, the suggested
         outlines and their grades then come from those frames only; the response is never shown,
         traces are hidden while choosing, and no suggestion is graded "responds". The frames end
         before the main response. A few cells can respond briefly inside them (1 to 4 cells per
         recording on the recordings checked); end the frames earlier to leave those out.
       - All frames: the average of all frames, which includes the frames after the stimulus.
       - All frames with the response in magenta: dim cells are then found only if they respond,
         which raises % responding. Use it for the size or timing of responses, not for % responding.
     In Step 2, Picture switches between All frames, Before response (blind; it asks which frames)
     and Response (the response in magenta). The line under the switch says which is on.
     The way cells were chosen is recorded with every measurement. "Before the response, blind" is recorded
     only if nothing of the response (magenta, a trace, Step 4) was shown before the outlines were
     final; outlines changed after that are recorded as not blind.
     "‹ Change recording" goes back to the first screen; "‹ Back" buttons move between steps.
  3. You choose the cells: draw around each one, or let it suggest outlines and click the real cells.
     For every cell it finds a background area right next to that cell and shows it in blue.
  4. It measures every cell and its background in every frame of the ORIGINAL recording and saves
     the tables.

The recording file is never changed.


WHAT YOU NEED
  - Fiji (https://fiji.sc). Fiji already contains Bio-Formats, which opens microscope files.
  - Nothing else for drawing cells by hand.
  - Optional: Cellpose, only for the "Suggest cells automatically" button. The plugin can set it up
    for you with one button (needs internet once and about 2.5 GB of disk space; see the end of
    this file).


INSTALL (once)
  1. Download  Install_Calcium_Plugin.groovy  from the release page.
  2. Drag it onto the Fiji bar and press Run in the editor window that opens. It downloads, checks
     and installs the plugin.
  3. Quit Fiji and start it again.

The plugin is now at  Plugins > Ca²⁺ Imaging (Wang Lab).  Later versions install from its
Install update button.

By hand instead: extract the plugin ZIP, copy its  scripts  folder into Fiji's own folder
(File > Show Folder > ImageJ) and restart Fiji. The menu then reads  Plugins > Ca2+ Imaging (Wang Lab).
The first time it starts, the plugin renames its file to the name with ² and ⁺, so after the next
restart the menu reads  Ca²⁺ Imaging (Wang Lab).

Earlier versions were at  Plugins > Calcium Imaging > Calcium Cells Step By Step.  The first time the
new version starts it moves itself; restart Fiji once more to see it at its new place. The old file
goes into  calcium-plugin-backups  inside Fiji's folder.

To try it without installing: drag Calcium_Cells_Step_By_Step.groovy onto the Fiji bar and press Run
in the editor window that opens.


HOW TO USE IT
  1. If the ROI Manager holds outlines from other work, the plugin asks about them when it starts.
     See "Outlines already in the ROI Manager" below.
  2. Choose  Plugins > Ca²⁺ Imaging (Wang Lab).
  3. In the first window pick the recording: one that is open in Fiji, or "Choose a file from disk...".
     For a file from disk, the file chooser opens as soon as you pick that entry (or press
     "Choose file…"), and the file's name is shown under the list.
     Pick the original recording, not a processed copy. Choose a folder for the results. Press Next.
  4. The colour picture opens on the left, and a window with one card per step stays on top at the
     right. Step 2 is open; click the title of another step to open it (the open one closes).
       - To click: press "Suggest cells automatically", wait (about 20 seconds on a computer with a
         graphics chip Cellpose can use, several minutes without one: see "How long the suggestions
         take"), then click once inside each outline that is a real cell. (If the window says Cellpose is not installed,
         press "Set up Cellpose (one time)" first; you can draw cells by hand while it installs.) The outlines are graded by what was measured:
             thick cyan    responds on its own after the stimulus
             thin cyan     likely a cell (not bright on average, brighter than its surroundings)
             grey dashes   unsure
             red           very bright: probably precipitate
         Move the mouse over an outline to read the measurements behind its grade and to see its
         gray value over time. "Accept likely" takes every cyan outline at once; go
         through them afterwards and remove the wrong ones.
       - To draw: press Oval or Freehand, draw around a cell, then press "Add outline" (or t).
         Drawing and clicking can be mixed freely.
       - Accepted cells have WHITE outlines with a thick BLACK outer border, distinct from all
         suggestion colours. Edge cells that are left out still use dashed outlines.
       - To remove a cell: click inside its white outline, then "Remove selected". The selected
         outline becomes thicker. This also works with suggestions hidden, or for hand-drawn cells.
       - To remove several cells quickly: turn on "Click cells to remove", then click inside each
         unwanted white outline. Turn it off before accepting more suggestions. Oval/Freehand also
         turns it off. Empty space and unaccepted suggestions are not removed or accepted in this mode.
       - "Undo" restores deleted cells or reverses additions (including Accept likely).
         Up to 20 plugin edits can be undone, preserving cell order and names. Edits made directly in
         the ROI Manager, including adding with t, clear this history rather than being overwritten.
         To remove a group, select several cells in the ROI Manager and press Remove selected.
     Each cell's background area appears in blue. Click a cell to see only its own background.
       - "Leave out cells within 40 px of the edge" is ticked at the start. While it is
         ticked, suggestions at the edge are not shown, a drawn outline at the edge is not added,
         and edge outlines already in the ROI Manager are drawn dashed and not measured. Untick it
         to treat edge cells like all others. See "Cells at the edge of the picture" below.
  5. Press "Continue to measure" (or click the Step 3 title), then "Measure and save". The window
     then shows how many cells were measured and the legacy 5-SD screening-rise count.
     That old screening count is not the protocol-defined responder percentage in Step 4.

HOW THE PICTURES AND NUMBERS ARE MADE
All of it uses Fiji's ordinary commands; the same steps can be done by hand from the menus.

  All-frames picture   Image > Stacks > Z Project (Sum Slices, all frames); Process > Math > Divide by
                       the number of frames; Process > Subtract Background (rolling ball 150);
                       Process > Filters > Gaussian Blur (sigma 1.5); shown 0 to 8.
  Response             The movie is cut into 24 x 24 pixel squares. If at least 5 % of the squares
                       step up in brightness within the same 15 frames, that moment is taken as the
                       response. Response picture = 20 frames around the peak divided by 20 frames
                       before it, Gaussian Blur sigma 2, shown 1.00 to 1.10. If no response is found,
                       the colour picture simply has no magenta.
  Colour picture       green = all-frames picture (0 to 8); red = the same picture shown 10 to 30, so
                       only very bright objects get red and turn yellow; magenta = response picture.
  Blind picture        The same steps as the all-frames picture on the frames before the response only
                       (Z Project Sum Slices of those frames, Divide, Subtract Background 150, Gaussian
                       Blur 1.5). Cellpose runs twice on it, never on the response: once shown from
                       0 to 2 gray levels and once from 0 to 8, brighter pixels capped. The 0-2 picture
                       keeps precipitate and rounded cells from flattening the dim cells; the 0-8 picture
                       keeps the brighter cells whole. Every outline from the 0-2 picture is kept. An
                       outline from the 0-8 picture is added when it shares less than 10 % of the
                       smaller outline's area with every 0-2 outline. Suggestions are graded on the
                       pre-stimulus picture (size, brightness, contrast).
                       With a graphics chip Cellpose also gets a third picture, shown 0-4 gray
                       levels; its outlines are added by the same rule against the outlines kept so
                       far (four audited recordings: 567 of 837 audited cells get an outline instead
                       of 547, for 6 % more outlines and about 18 s). Without a graphics chip it is
                       not run.
                       Saved in automatic_outlines_blind/: one folder per picture
                       (picture_1_shown_0_to_2_gray_levels, picture_2_shown_0_to_8_gray_levels and,
                       with a graphics chip, picture_3_shown_0_to_4_gray_levels) with
                       the picture, Cellpose's outlines and messages; detection_picture.txt (how many
                       outlines came from each picture); ..._suggestions_rois.zip (all suggestions,
                       named as in the table); and the table, whose last column says which picture
                       each suggestion came from.
                       Tested on four ATP recordings of 18 Nov 2026: of the response-assisted outlines
                       that could be cells, the two pictures together found 68 / 95 / 55 / 62 %
                       (C2 Run1 / C3 Run2 / P2 Run1 / P3 Run2), against 46 / 94 / 34 / 50 % with 0-2
                       alone and 57 / 86 / 45 / 36 % with 0-8 alone. No single cap was best on all
                       four. The cost is 25-55 % more suggestions to review.
  Order in Step 2      The blue "Next:" line at the top of Step 2 always names the one thing to do:
                       Suggest cells, Accept likely, click the dashed outlines that are cells, then
                       Add cells that respond… and Continue to measure. In a recording with a response,
                       Add cells that respond… is the highlighted step after the blind cells: blind
                       selection alone leaves cells that only show when they respond without an outline
                       (P2 Run1 ATP: 65 blind cells, 92 more with the response shown). Continue to
                       measure with blind cells only asks once whether to add them first, and after a
                       blind-only measurement Step 3 has the same button beside Back to cells.
  Blind, then magenta  Blind and response-assisted choosing can be used together: choose cells blind, then
                       press Add cells that respond… (the same as switching Picture to Response
                       (magenta)); the plugin then looks for the cells that are only visible when
                       they respond, by itself. Every cell is recorded as chosen blind, or as added (or reshaped)
                       after the response was shown (..._cells_chosen_blind_or_after_response.csv). When
                       Step 4 finishes it gives % responding for all cells first, then for each group. Only
                       the blind group is free of selection on the response: the response picture can add
                       dim cells that respond, never dim cells that do not. A further line gives the % among
                       quiet cells (required rise 30 % or less), and a switch leaves out the cells whose
                       corrected fluorescence went below zero. All of it is saved in
                       ..._percent_responding_<analysis>.csv. A response must hold for 2 samples in a row.
  Grades               responds = the outline rises on its own at the stimulus (more than 0.5 gray levels,
                       1.25 times its surroundings, and 5 % of its own brightness), also when part of it
                       is very bright; likely = dim, even, clearly brighter than its surroundings;
                       precipitate? = very bright and no rise of its own; unsure = the rest.
  Show                 One switch per kind drawn on the picture (your cells, likely a cell, unsure,
                       background, precipitate?, responds). Click one to hide or show that kind;
                       double-click to see only that kind (again for everything).
  The four actions     Suggest cells, Accept likely, Add cells that respond... and Continue to measure keep
                       their places. The blue one is the next to press; a gray one cannot be pressed now.
  Step 4 start values  The frames confirmed for blind selection become the baseline and the stimulus is
                       taken to follow their last frame (more than 30 frames: the last 20 are the resting
                       baseline). Otherwise the frames before the rise the plugin found. The settings
                       last used for a recording are filled in again next time (step4_last_settings.json).
  Step 4 page          The plot opens on the Average: the mean trace (with SEM) of all cells, or of the
                       group chosen under Show, and nothing else. The buttons above it switch to
                       "One cell + average" or "Responders vs non-responders".
                       Signal: dF/F0, gray levels above background, or raw gray values (not corrected).
                       Cell box: empty (no cell selected; the page opens that way) or a cell. Type its
                       name, or its number and Enter, or pick it from the list, or click the cell in
                       the picture. No cell again: the box's ✕, a click on empty space in the picture,
                       or Esc outside a box. Esc while typing in the box puts back the open cell.
                       A double-click on the picture shows all of it and keeps the cell.
                       With a cell selected the plot shows that cell over the average, with its required
                       rise as a dashed line. Thresholds adds the threshold that did not set the required
                       rise (the minimum rise or the noise threshold) and the baseline mean. Every line and
                       band is named once, in the legend; the plot itself carries one word, "stimulus".
                       View: Recording, Background removed, GFP (the same in green), Response in
                       magenta (GFP plus the rise above what the background did, averaged over
                       9 x 9 pixels and 5 frames), Change from baseline (that rise alone), Colour
                       overview and Background areas. Outline thickness is beside "All outlines".
                       Outlines: By call (each outline in the colour of its response call) or Plain
                       (every outline white; excluded and unscorable cells stay dashed). Number cells
                       writes each cell's number in its outline. Saved pictures and frames follow both.
                       Zooming the picture: + and - on its top left corner, with the zoom level between
                       them; Whole picture; and Zoom to region (then drag a rectangle; Esc cancels;
                       Shift + drag does the same). With the pointer on the picture the keys +, - and 0
                       do the same, and a pinch or Ctrl or Cmd + scroll zooms about the pointer. Zoomed
                       in, drag the picture to move it, or click or drag in the small map on its
                       bottom right corner. Scrolling without Ctrl or Cmd scrolls the page.
                       The plot's time axis: Ctrl or Cmd + scroll zooms it, a double-click resets it.
                       Over the plot, the time and the plotted values under the pointer are shown beside a
                       dashed line; they go when the pointer leaves. While the frame moves (a drag in the
                       plot, the slider, the step buttons, the arrow keys, Play) a solid line shows where
                       the picture is, with that frame's numbers; it goes a moment after the frame stops.
                       Save frames... (Export) writes every Nth frame and one picture with all of them,
                       or, with Baseline and peak, one picture of those two frames side by side.
                       The arrow on the edge of the settings panel folds the panel away.
                       Each panel has one Export menu in its corner (the picture, the plot, the heatmap
                       and the tables). In it, Save plot puts the picture, its numbers (CSV) and how it
                       was made into a "plots" folder beside the tables, Download hands the same file to
                       the browser. For the plot and the heatmap, Edit figure opens the figure with a
                       preview that is the saved picture itself, and beside it: width and height in
                       inches, resolution and format; title, axis titles, font (Arial unless you choose
                       another), text sizes and colour; for the plot the axis minimum, maximum and tick
                       spacing, frame, grid lines, and the thickness and colour of each line; for the
                       heatmap its colours and a colour scale you can type so several recordings share
                       one. A number is taken when you press Enter or leave its box. One the figure
                       cannot use stays in the box, marked, and the line under it says what the box
                       takes ("1 to 20 in", "Minimum must be below maximum", "Too large for this
                       figure size"); the figure keeps the number it had. Text that leaves the plot
                       less than 40 % of the figure is said under Text size. A title too long for the
                       figure is shortened with an ellipsis; a legend entry goes on two lines. With All
                       traces on, Lines also sets the traces' opacity in percent (empty: auto, fainter
                       the more cells there are) and the responders' line. Size, font, colours and
                       thicknesses are kept for the next recording, and the editor says in one line
                       when kept settings are in use; the title and the axis numbers are not kept.
                       Reset to defaults puts every box of the editor back to its built-in value,
                       resolution and format too. For pictures and frames, Options
                       sets the format (PNG or TIFF), the width in inches, the resolution (150, 300 or
                       600 dpi, recorded in the file) and the TIFF compression (LZW, ZIP or none; LZW
                       and ZIP lose nothing). Format and resolution are the figures' too.
                       Save picture saves the view on screen with the outlines shown and a colour key;
                       Save frames… saves every Nth frame of a movie view, each labelled with its
                       frame and time. Area, in the picture's Export menu and in Save frames…, chooses
                       what they show: Whole picture, however the picture is zoomed, or Area on screen,
                       the part of the recording in the picture panel. A picture of an area is as wide
                       as one of the whole field; its outlines, numbers and labels keep their size,
                       and it has a scale bar (when the recording gives its pixel size in micrometres).
                       Its file name says "area", and the file kept beside it gives the area in pixels
                       and in µm. Save frames… › Baseline and peak saves one picture: the baseline
                       frame on the left, the peak frame on the right, both at the same brightness,
                       with the key and one scale bar under the pair. Its two frame boxes are filled
                       in (the last F₀ frame; the selected cell's peak, or the frame where the cells
                       that responded are brightest on average) and can be changed. Signal also offers
                       dF/F0 of the raw values. View: the recording as recorded, with the background
                       removed, or with the response in magenta (green cells turn pink as they rise;
                       display only). Drag the edges between the settings, the movie and the plot to
                       resize them. "Show explanations" in the header turns the help texts on.
  Sensitivity check    After a blind measurement (tick box in Step 3, on by default) the response-
                       assisted detection runs once for comparison (saved outlines are reused). The
                       report says how many of its outlines that could be cells (graded responds,
                       likely or unsure) the blind detection also found, by grade. A response-assisted
                       outline counts as found when a blind outline contains its centre or it contains
                       the centre of a blind outline. Its outlines are never added to the cells or
                       measured. Files: ..._sensitivity_check.csv and
                       ..._sensitivity_check_missed_RoiSet.zip (the missed ones that could be cells; open it in the
                       ROI Manager). If most are
                       found, the blind picture picks up dim cells well. If many are missed, % responding
                       is still unbiased only if cells that are dim at rest respond at the same rate as
                       the others: state that assumption with the result.
  Background of a cell A band from 6 to 40 pixels outside the cell's outline, without other cells
                       (widened by 4 pixels) and without bright objects. Of the pixels left, the
                       darkest quarter (ranked in the all-frames picture) is that cell's background.
  Measuring            Mean gray value of the cell and of its background in every frame of the
                       original recording. corrected = raw - background.
  Around each cell     In the same pass, for the checks on the calls: the mean gray value of 25 fixed
                       sets of pixels per cell. Near ring: outside the outline, more than 0.645 and up
                       to 6.45 micrometres from it. Far ring: beyond that, up to 14.19 micrometres. Each
                       ring is cut into 8 sectors of 45 degrees: right, lower right, bottom, lower left,
                       left, upper left, top, upper right, seen from the mean position of the outline's
                       pixels. Both rings leave out pixels within 1.29 micrometres of another measured
                       outline and keep bright pixels. Core: the pixels inside that lie deeper than the
                       median depth. Rim: the other pixels inside, by sector. Distances are between
                       pixel centres; the picture's border counts as outside. A set under 12 pixels is
                       not used. At 0.645 micrometres per pixel the widths are 1, 10, 22 and 2 pixels,
                       which is how they were set. A recording without a pixel size in micrometres,
                       or with pixels that are not square, takes these pixel values, and
                       what_was_done.txt says which. These numbers are a record of their own: no
                       cell value, background or call is made from them. If they cannot be saved,
                       everything else is saved as before and what_was_done.txt names the file.


HOW THE SUGGESTIONS ARE GRADED
Every suggested outline is measured: its size, its mean brightness, the share of very bright pixels
in it, how much brighter it is than a ring around it, and how much it rises after the stimulus
compared with that ring. Very bright outlines are graded "probably precipitate". Dim outlines that
rise more than their surroundings are graded "responds". Dim, smooth outlines that stand out from
their surroundings are graded "likely a cell". So is, since 2026.11.20.20, a cell-sized outline
(500 pixels or more) that stands out by more than 0.3 gray levels, is not brighter than 8 gray
levels on average and has less than 60 % very bright pixels: a cell with a bright dot or bright
patches inside. The rest are "unsure".
On the blind suggestions of four audited recordings (837 audited cells) "Accept likely" took the
outlines of 232 audited cells before this rule and 360 with it; 73 % of the outlines it accepted
were on audited cells before, 71 % now. So about three in ten accepted outlines are still not
audited cells: look through them and remove the wrong ones.
On four recordings whose outlines had been reviewed one by one (1396 outlines), the share that
reviewers accepted as cells was: responds 83 %, likely 80 %, unsure 41 %, probably precipitate 12 %.
So the grades help, but about one in five cyan outlines is still not a cell. You decide.

HOW LONG THE SUGGESTIONS TAKE
Cellpose is fast only with a graphics chip it can use: the chip in Apple-silicon Macs, or an NVIDIA
card on Windows or Linux. Intel and AMD built-in graphics do not count.
  - With such a chip: about 20 seconds for a 1388 x 1040 picture.
  - Without one, Cellpose works on the processor. Measured on a 10-core Apple processor with the
    chip switched off: 7.5 minutes at full size. The plugin therefore gives the picture at half
    size on such computers ("--diameter 60"): 2.8 minutes on the same processor. An office PC is
    slower than that. You can keep drawing cells by hand while it works.
  - Half size against full size, on a recording with 210 reviewed cells: half size put an outline
    over 192 of them, full size over 184. The outlines are about 7 % larger in area and are not
    the same set (250 of 300 match a full-size outline). The grade percentages above were measured
    on full-size outlines only.
  - The button shows what is happening: the model download the first time (in MB), then the seconds.
  - Outlines are saved in the results folder (automatic_outlines). When the same recording is opened
    again with the same results folder, they are loaded in a second instead of being made again;
    "Suggest cells again" makes new ones. If the results folder is shared between computers
    (OneDrive, a network drive), outlines made on a fast computer should be picked up on a slow
    one; this has not been tried between two computers yet.

CELLS AT THE EDGE OF THE PICTURE
A cell closer to the border than the background band reaches (40 pixels) can only get its
background from the inner side. The lamp is usually weaker toward the edge, so that background is
measured under more light than the cell, and the cell comes out too dim or even darker than its
background. On four recordings with reviewed outlines, cells within 40 pixels of the edge sat 0.5
to 2.3 gray levels lower above their background than cells further in, and were 2 to 7 times as
likely to be less than 1 gray level above it. On one recording with 120 chosen cells, leaving the
14 edge cells out cut the cells at or below background from 5 to 1 (percent responding 74.5 %
against 72.5 % with them).
The tick box is on by default. It costs cells: about one outline in five touches that 40-pixel
border zone. The summary table always has a column saying whether a cell is within 40 pixels of
the edge, and the record of steps says whether edge cells were left out. Change the distance with
EDGE_MARGIN in the settings block.

OUTLINES ALREADY IN THE ROI MANAGER
Fiji has one ROI Manager. Outlines left in it from another recording would be measured on the next
one. The plugin notes on which recording it added each outline. The notes are kept beside the ROI
Manager, not in the outlines, and last until the ROI Manager or Fiji is closed. Saved RoiSet files
are as before.
When the plugin starts and the ROI Manager already holds outlines, it asks once, before any cell
is shown:
    Clear them                    the default. These outlines are removed; Undo brings them back.
    Keep them for this recording  only when this recording shows the same cells in the same place.
    Cancel                        nothing starts, and the outlines stay as they were.
Clear them takes effect only when the start goes on: Cancel at the question about the frames
leaves the outlines as they were.
It does not ask when the ROI Manager is empty, when the outlines were made on this same
recording, for example after its plugin window was closed and the plugin started on it again, or
when they were drawn on this recording's own window before the plugin started. The same recording
means the same size, number of frames, and first, middle and last frame, whatever its name, time
base or pixel size.
Outlines opened from a RoiSet file before the plugin starts are outlines from before: unless the
plugin added them while this ROI Manager was open, it cannot tell which recording they are from, so
it asks.
To measure outlines saved from this recording, open them in the ROI Manager once the plugin window
is open on it. They are then this recording's own, as before.

Kept outlines were not chosen on this recording, so they are recorded as carried over and never
as chosen blind:
  - in _what_was_done.txt: an "Outlines carried over" line and a "Carried over" line, and the
    line "Chosen blind or later" counts them separately;
  - in _cells_chosen_blind_or_after_response.csv: "carried over from" and the recording's name;
  - in the ROI registry and in the cell selection basis;
  - in Step 4: a row of their own in the percent table, "Carried over". They are not counted
    under Chosen blind.
Outlines drawn on this recording's own window before the plugin started are kept without a
question. They were not chosen in the plugin either: the record says "drawn before the plugin
started", and Step 4 counts them under Carried over.
With an empty ROI Manager at the start and the recording left open, every saved file is as before.

"‹ Change recording" asks its own question: Clear outlines and go back, Keep outlines and go back,
or Cancel. On another recording, kept outlines are recorded as carried over, never as chosen
blind; on the same recording they stay its own. The next start does not ask about them a second
time.

Measure and save checks again. If the ROI Manager holds outlines the plugin added on another
recording that were not kept for this one, it asks the same question before measuring. Cancel
measures nothing and says why. Clear removes those outlines and measures nothing yet, so that the
cells can be checked first; Undo brings them back. Keep measures them as carried over.

ONE PLUGIN WINDOW
The plugin has one window. Starting it from the menu while that window is open brings the window
forward and offers Change recording, instead of opening a second window. With the ROI Manager
empty it asks "Go back to choose another recording?". Starting it while another start is still
under way, for example while the first start makes its pictures, opens nothing and says "The
plugin is already starting. Finish or cancel that start first." To work on another recording, use
Change recording.

THE RECORDING IS CLOSED
"The recording is closed in Fiji. Open it again to go on." means that the recording's own window
was closed in Fiji while the plugin window stayed open. Its frames are no longer in memory, so
nothing that reads them can run: Measure and save, the picture of other frames before the response,
looking for the response, a cell's trace and the movie in Step 4. Each one shows this sentence
instead, also when the recording is closed while it runs, and nothing is saved or changed. Suggest
cells automatically shows it too when pressed while the recording is closed. Pictures already made,
such as All frames or a response already found, can still be shown. The Recording card says it too,
in red. The cells chosen so far stay as they are.
To go on, press "Open the recording again" on the Recording card, or open the same file in Fiji.
The button is shown only while Fiji knows the recording's file and the file is there. The plugin
goes on only with the same recording: the same size, number of frames, time base, pixel size, and
first, middle and last frame. Another recording with the same name is not taken. When it cannot go
on, the card says why:
    The file is no longer there: with the file's path
    The file could not be opened.
    The file did not open as the same recording.
    It did not come from a file, so it cannot be opened again.
When the cells are measured again, _what_was_done.txt says that the recording was closed and opened
again.
Step 4 goes on with it too, also a Step 4 page or window opened while it was closed.

DIM CELLS AND PERCENT RESPONDING
Some corrected resting fluorescence is zero or negative, so a fractional change cannot be
interpreted. Step 4 keeps these cells as technical exclusions with reasons; missing or insufficient
local stimulus-window coverage is unscorable. The reported percentage is 100 * Responded / Evaluable,
where Evaluable = Responded + Did not respond. Registered, excluded and unscorable counts remain
separate, and accepted edge exclusions remain in the ROI registry/cohort flow.
Low F0 relative to baseline SD and zero SD are review warnings, not automatic exclusions.
For a noise-only rule, zero estimated SD is unscorable unless a justified nonzero noise floor is set.
The old intensity-lifted table adds an arbitrary constant to dim traces. That changes fractional
amplitudes and thresholds; it does not repair normalization or provide a valid responder denominator.
It remains available for legacy diagnostics. The Step 3 screening-rise flag is a separate historical
5-SD display aid, not the user-declared Step 4 rule.
Choose cells independently of whether they respond; neither normalization nor thresholds can undo
response-selected ROI sampling. Supply independent culture/animal/preparation IDs when known;
cell counts and fields are not automatically independent biological replicates.


LIMITS: PLEASE READ
  - The settings are in pixels and were chosen on recordings of 1388 x 1040 pixels at 0.645
    micrometres per pixel (cultured cells loaded with Fluo-4, one frame per second). For a different
    magnification or camera, change the numbers in the "settings" block at the top of the .groovy
    file (it is a plain text file).
  - Background choice changes the resting level. For dim cells, inspect the raw reference,
    background and quality flags; an unstable reference makes fractional amplitudes difficult to
    interpret. Raw rises and baseline noise help review the recording, but shifting a trace upward
    does not repair normalization. Historical "cell_above_background" flags are review aids, not a
    universal cell-eligibility threshold.
  - The automatic response finder misses a response when only a handful of cells respond.
  - The suggested outlines are suggestions only. They include precipitate and sometimes join two
    cells. Nothing becomes a cell unless you click it.
  - The original imaging workflow was tested on one Mac with Fiji (ImageJ 1.54p). Automated
    Windows/Linux interface checks and Windows/macOS/Linux R checks do not establish biological
    accuracy or replace a complete imaging-workflow check on the computer being used.

CELLPOSE FOR THE "SUGGEST CELLS" BUTTON
Cellpose is a free program, separate from Fiji (https://github.com/MouseLand/cellpose). It is not
part of Fiji and cannot be added from Fiji's update sites; it is a Python program that the plugin
starts in the background.

The easy way: the button
If the plugin window says "Cellpose is not installed on this computer yet", press
"Set up Cellpose (one time)". It first shows what it will do and asks for your OK. Then it:
  1. gets "uv", a small free installer (about 17 MB, from github.com/astral-sh/uv), unless uv is
     already on the computer, and compares the file with its published checksum;
  2. makes a private Python 3.11 with Cellpose in it (about 250 MB to download and 1 GB on disk
     on a Mac, more on Linux; from github.com/astral-sh/python-build-standalone and pypi.org);
  3. checks that Cellpose starts.
On Windows and Linux the installer is asked to pick the version that can use an NVIDIA graphics
card when the computer has one (a much bigger download in that case). That part is not yet tested.
The first press of "Suggest cells automatically" then downloads Cellpose's model (about 1.2 GB).
The setup itself took under a minute on a fast connection; the model download takes longer.
You can go on drawing cells by hand while it runs.

Where it goes: two folders in your home folder,  cellpose-env  and  cellpose-env-tools , and the
model in a third one called  .cellpose . Nothing else on the computer is changed. To remove
Cellpose again, delete those folders. If the setup fails, the reason is in
cellpose-env-tools/setup_log.txt (a common cause is a network that blocks downloads).

How far this was tested: on one Mac with an Apple chip (19 Nov 2026), the setup was run for real
into an empty folder, with every download: the installer (checksum matched), Python 3.11.18 and
Cellpose 4.2.1.1 with 24 other packages. It finished in 46 seconds, and the Cellpose it made gave
the same 284 outlines on a test recording as an existing installation. Not yet tried: clicking
the button on screen rather than from a test program, the model download on a computer that has
never run Cellpose, an Intel Mac and Linux. On Windows the button has been used once, on a
Windows 11 PC with Intel built-in graphics: it installed, and Cellpose started and ran on the
processor (slowly, see "How long the suggestions take").

By hand instead, in a terminal:

    uv venv --python 3.11 ~/cellpose-env
    uv pip install --python ~/cellpose-env/bin/python cellpose packaging

(uv is a free Python installer: https://docs.astral.sh/uv . The word "packaging" matters: without
it Cellpose 4.2 installs but stops with "No module named 'packaging'". Installing with conda into
an environment called "cellpose" also works.)

If Cellpose is already installed somewhere else: the plugin looks in ~/cellpose-env and in conda
environments named "cellpose". Otherwise press "Suggest cells automatically" and choose "I already
have it: show where", then pick the "python" file of the environment in which Cellpose is
installed; the plugin remembers it. You can also write that path as one line in a text file called
cellpose_python.txt  next to the plugin file (scripts > Plugins in Fiji's folder).



WINDOW SIZE AND SCROLLING
The plugin window can be resized. Smaller windows show scrollbars instead of compressing the
controls. Use the mouse wheel or vertical scrollbar to reach lower sections; use the horizontal
scrollbar when the window is too narrow. Detection, results, and update messages preserve the
window size you chose. The initial window fits within the screen. The colour picture is fitted
into the free space beside the plugin window. The native Step 4 fallback can open beside Fiji;
the default Step 4 page uses an ordinary browser window.

STEP 2 PROGRESS AND GITHUB UPDATES
The progress panel shows percentage stage progress, a persistent explanation of the current stage,
total and stage time, image dimensions, CPU/GPU, attempt number, detection settings, download
percentage when supplied, and the count of outlines checked. Details opens a scrollable
history of recent events and Cellpose messages. Saved-outline reuse is clearly labelled. Quiet
periods keep the stage explanation and show whether the Cellpose process is still running. It advances when
work or reported stages complete, not merely as time passes. Cellpose can be silent during
inference. Use Log or Cancel during long runs. The bar reaches 100%
only after the outlines are read, graded, and saved. Setup is a separate operation and disables
cancellation until installation returns.

Saved outlines are reused only for the same detection picture and when no interrupted-run marker
is present. Suggest cells again starts a fresh run. Failed or cancelled runs never become reusable
results. Edge-cell controls and the faster CPU settings from this version are preserved.

STEP 4 SETTINGS AND QUALITY REVIEW (2026.11.20.5)
Invalid settings show inline errors and must be corrected before analysis. Rule types keep
separate drafts; Discard pending changes restores the completed-run settings, including files.
A positive control needs its actual timing entered. Review the suggested primary onset and
keep the inclusive F0 baseline before stimulation; overlap produces a visible warning.
Quality warnings are a button in the row of result figures ("19 cells flagged"). Its list has a row
for every flagged cell with its call and its flags; a click on a row opens that cell, and the left
and right arrow keys then step through the flagged cells. Warnings also show as
a line on the plot card under the selected cell's call, with a Needs quality review entry under
Show. Warnings do not automatically exclude cells or change saved calls.
Where things are since 2026.11.20.19: a warning or error about a setting sits under that setting, and
the Run box only says how many there are ("1 warning above"; click it to go there). The result figures
at the top are figures only; Show, beside Cell, is the one cell filter.
Whole-recording AUC, peak timestamp and rise time are labelled separately from the
response-window metrics. See docs/SIMPLECA.md for the definitions.
The tables name each column in words with its unit; pointing at a header gives its saved name,
pointing at a number the saved value. Saved CSVs keep every column under its saved name.
Each measurement has a separate review page and preserved inputs. Keep Fiji open; you may
close the cell-selection window. Close analysis releases that page's temporary inputs.
